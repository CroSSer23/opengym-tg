About this fork

openGym is somebody else's app. This page is about which parts are, and which parts are not.

Whose work this is

openGym was written by Duarte Santos, who built it for himself, kept using it, and open-sourced the whole thing under the AGPL. Nearly everything you see is his: the guided workout flow, the progression engine, the muscle map, the importers, the twelve translations, the exercise library work. The licence is the reason this fork can exist at all.

The AI Coach came next, in a fork by Alex Costa: the intake, the proposal flow, the payload allowlist, the validator, and the Coach/Engine boundary that keeps a language model away from the arithmetic. That boundary is the good idea in this whole feature and it is not mine.

This fork adds four things on top, and nothing else:

  • A Telegram Mini App. The tracker inside Telegram, signed in by the Telegram account that opened it, with the app's notifications delivered as messages.
  • An endpoint-driven Coach. A provider that posts to any OpenAI-compatible /chat/completions, so the Coach is not tied to a vendor CLI or an account with one particular company.
  • The rest of the roadmap. 5/3/1 percentage programming, three more starter plans, body measurements, a plate calculator, per-exercise notes.
  • A redesign. The app used to be an iOS system app in look: Apple's palette, Apple's grouped lists, Apple's type. It now reads as a training ledger, with the numbers you are actually reading set at the size you are actually reading them.

Why fork rather than contribute

Most of the above would be a reasonable pull request and some of it may become one. A Telegram Mini App is a different question: it adds a second sign-in path and a dependency on one company's client to a project whose entire argument is that it depends on nobody. That is a decision for the person who maintains openGym, not for someone passing through, so it lives here instead.

The licence

AGPL-3.0, the same as upstream, and not by accident. If you run a modified copy as a network service, you owe your users the source of the version you are running. That is why the source link is in the footer of every page here and why this fork is public.

Nothing on this site is affiliated with or endorsed by the upstream authors. If something here is broken, it is almost certainly a change made in this fork, so open an issue here rather than upstream.
