What this fork adds

Three things upstream does not do

Everything else here is openGym as its author built it. These are the parts this fork exists for.

It runs inside Telegram

The whole tracker as a Mini App, opened from your own bot. Your Telegram account is the sign-in, so there is nothing to create and nothing to remember, and rest timers, workout reminders and coach proposals arrive as messages. On iOS that is the only notification channel a Mini App has.

The AI coach runs on your endpoint

Point it at anything that speaks /chat/completions: OpenAI, OpenRouter, a LiteLLM or vLLM gateway, or an Ollama on your own network. No vendor CLI, no account with anybody in particular.

5/3/1, and the rest of the roadmap

Percentage programming off a training max, three more starter plans, body measurements, a plate calculator and per-exercise notes. All of it was on the upstream roadmap and none of it was written yet.

The AI designs the plan. It never picks the weight.

The split is the whole design, and it is the reason an AI is safe to have here at all. Judgement and arithmetic live in different places, and the model never crosses into the arithmetic.

Where each decision is made
The Coach, where judgement livesThe Engine, where the maths lives
Writes your weekly routinesComputes tomorrow's bar weight
Chooses a progression ruleApplies it, the same way every time
Reads your effort ratings and stallsCounts hits and misses strictly
Swaps an exercise that is not workingOwns every load you actually lift

If the endpoint times out or the provider disappears, the engine carries on offline without missing a session. Proposals are inert until you accept them one by one, applying takes a snapshot first, and one tap puts it back.

Five rules, and every target says why

Pick one per routine, override it per exercise. Your weights are already right when the session opens.

5/3/1 runs the table below off a training max, about 90% of a real one. Work weights round down, because a percentage is a ceiling. The last set of the first three weeks is taken for as many reps as you have.

One 5/3/1 cycle, as percentages of the training max
WeekSet 1Set 2Top set
165% × 575% × 585% × 5+
270% × 380% × 390% × 3+
375% × 585% × 395% × 1+
440% × 550% × 560% × 5

Then the training max goes up and the cycle starts again. The week you are in is counted from your log, so correcting a mistyped session moves the cycle with it.

One command, and it is yours

Docker with Compose is the only requirement. The frontend builds inside the image, so you do not need Node, and the first run downloads the exercise media once.

Your data lives in ./data on your own disk: profiles, plans, every logged set, your weight. Back up that folder and you have backed up everything.

Self-host it 
git clone https://github.com/CroSSer23/opengym-tg
cd opengym-tg
cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d

Open http://localhost:8080 and create a profile. For passkeys and for the Telegram Mini App you need an HTTPS domain, which is two lines in .env.

The tracker underneath

All of this is upstream openGym, which is most of what makes the app worth self-hosting in the first place.

Guided workouts It knows what day it is, pre-fills your weights from last time, times your rest and spots PRs.

1,324 exercises Searchable, with animated demos, filtered by the equipment you actually own. Add your own.

Weekly plan A routine per weekday, and any day can be moved when life happens.

Body weight and goal A chart with your goal line, every change coloured by whether it moves toward it.

Muscle map A body diagram shaded by how much work each muscle got, and which ones got none.

Activity heatmap A year of training at a glance, shaded by time spent.

Estimated 1RM Per exercise, from your best eligible set, and it names which one.

Supersets and cardio Pair lifts back to back with one rest, and log time and speed where reps make no sense.

Effort per set RIR or RPE, whichever scale you already think in. Optional.

Timed sets Planks, hangs and carries logged by time, with a timer for the hold itself.

Bring your history Import from FitNotes, Strong and Hevy, or body weight from an Apple Health export.

Passkeys, not passwords Face ID or a fingerprint, and a separate profile per person in the house.

AGPL, and a fork that says so

openGym was written by Duarte Santos, who open-sourced it under the AGPL. The AI Coach came from a fork by Alex Costa. This is a further fork: the Telegram Mini App, the endpoint-driven Coach, the redesign and the remaining roadmap items are the changes made here. Everything else is their work, and the licence keeps all of it open.
